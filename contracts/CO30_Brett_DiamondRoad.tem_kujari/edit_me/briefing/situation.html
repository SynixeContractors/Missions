A ceasefire has been negotiated between Khaybara and the Southern Independence Movement, temporarily halting large-scale fighting while both sides discuss political terms. The front line remains tense, and military forces are withdrawing north of the river to comply with the truce. The south remains volatile, however, with local militias, disgruntled community groups, and opportunistic armed civilians still operating in the area. EMPLOYER Name: Khaybara Government The government has agreed to the ceasefire and is withdrawing forces north of the river in order to pursue negotiations. Khaybara is interested in the safe extraction of IDAP personnel and the recovery of critical records that could compromise negotiations or reveal political and military information. ENEMY FORCES Name: Hostile Militias / Anti-IDAP Groups Location: South of the River These hostile groups are not formal military units, but are armed and organised enough to intimidate, blockade, and threaten IDAP personnel. They are motivated by anti-government sentiment and hostility toward humanitarian groups they believe are aligned with Khaybara. The groups are not necessarily seeking a conventional battle; they are more likely to harass, threaten, block movement, and escalate violence if provoked or if they perceive the extraction team as a threat. FRIENDLY FORCES Name: Khaybara Military Location: North of the River Friendly government forces have withdrawn to the north and are not expected to provide direct support in the south under the ceasefire. Their primary role is to maintain the northern side of the agreement and prepare for follow-on security operations after the extraction. Contractors are not to assume direct military support. INDEPENDENT FORCES Name: Southern Independence Movement Location: South of the River The Southern Independence Movement is neutral to Synixe operations and has granted limited permission for contractors to move in their areas of control for the purpose of IDAP personnel extraction. The movement is not hostile to the team so long as operations remain within the agreed window and do not appear to threaten southern political interests. Contractors are expected to follow southern military instructions while in the area and to avoid unnecessary escalation. CIVILIAN CONSIDERATIONS Name: Local Civilian Population Location: South of the River The local civilian population is mixed in sentiment and may be fearful, uneasy, or hostile toward IDAP staff. Some residents see the relief workers as legitimate humanitarian actors, while others view them as government-aligned and therefore a threat. Civilian hostility is likely to manifest as intimidation, protests, or obstruction rather than outright combat. Contractors should avoid towns and populated routes when possible and use warning shots before escalating force when feasible.
