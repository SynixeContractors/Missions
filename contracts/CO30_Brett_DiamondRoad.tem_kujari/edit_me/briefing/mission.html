A ceasefire has been agreed between Khaybara and the Southern Independence Movement, allowing a pause in hostilities while both sides begin political negotiations. Khaybara military forces have withdrawn north of the river to comply with the truce. Unfortunately, several local groups and militias remain hostile to the presence of IDAP medical personnel, viewing them as extensions of the government and therefore a legitimate target for intimidation or violence. A field medical centre operated by IDAP is still active south of the river and has become the focus of hostile pressure. Medical staff are at risk of being targeted by anti-government agitators, militias, and local civilians who believe the organisation is complicit in the conflict. Khaybara has contracted Synixe to enter the southern area during the ceasefire window, extract the medical workers, recover critical IDAP records, and return north before the truce breaks down. As agreed to by the southern forces, the operation is limited to a 120-minute window once the team crosses the river. IDAP has confirmed all staff were accounted for this morning, and patients have been sent home, although day walk-in services were still active. Local military forces may impose their own restrictions on movement or access. Contractors are expected to operate carefully, avoid town centres, and de-escalate where possible before resorting to force. IDAP has agreed to the armed extraction given the risk to its personnel, and has provided a list of staff to be recovered, although request that weapons be kept holstered or in vehicles while at the field hospital unless immediate danger requires otherwise.
